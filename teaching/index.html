Teaching Approach

Process, iteration, and evaluation over demonstration

My courses are built around the principle that students learn a medium by making something that behaves, and by encountering the consequences of their decisions. In production courses this means short demonstrations followed by studio time, frequent small experiments rather than a single high-stakes assignment, and documentation of iterations including the ones that failed.

Where the subject allows, I ask students to test their assumptions against real users rather than against their own intentions. In game and interaction design, this is the difference between a project that runs and a project that communicates, and it is what I assess.

I bring critical perspectives into production courses rather than treating them as separate from making. Students in game and media courses examine representation, surveillance, accessibility, and the politics of interface design alongside technical work, drawing on scholars including Safiya Noble, Lisa Nakamura, and Tara McPherson.

Online and Classroom Technology

UR Courses (Moodle), Zoom, and lab hardware

I have delivered CS 408 fully online, and used UR Courses (Moodle) extensively across all courses for content delivery, readings, discussion forums, assignment submission, and grading. I use Zoom for synchronous sessions, office hours, and student consultation, and structure asynchronous modules with recorded lectures, curated media, and discussion activities so that students who cannot attend live can engage fully.

In lab-based courses I manage classroom technologies directly, including hardware setup and troubleshooting for Arduino-based physical computing, and software configuration for Unity, Maya, and multimedia production tools.
