Selected Work

Left-Handed Inclusion in Computer Games

Doctoral research · design thinking from empathise to test

Problem
Control schemes in commercial games are built around right-handed defaults, excluding left-handed players from comfortable, precise play.
Method
Exploratory study of social media posts by left-handed players; definition of the problem and construction of the LINC framework; ideation through Schell's lenses and established control-configuration principles; mirrored control schemes (left-handed control configuration and right-handed control configuration) as candidate interventions.
Prototype
MegaByte Meltdown, built from scratch in Unity: three levels, enemy AI behaviours, level design, and deployment to WebGL and Android.
Usability testing
Two between-subjects usability studies with 100 participants in total, using PENS, IMI, MSS, PA, and UC/UnC, a bespoke Player Video Game Engagement questionnaire, and an Interactive Fitts's Law Test measuring interaction efficiency across control conditions. Analysis in SPSS and jamovi.
Scope
Interface design and evaluation across desktop keyboard, mobile touchscreen, and browser deployment, with VR, AR, and adaptive controllers addressed in the literature review.

VisionAR: Impairment Simulator

Web based accessibility tool · desktop and mobile AR

Problem
Designers and educators rarely experience the visual conditions their users live with, so accessibility decisions are made abstractly. A simulator makes the consequences of a design visible before it ships.
Work
A browser based camera experience simulating five visual conditions: normal vision, macular degeneration, tunnel vision, cataracts, and achromatopsia, with an adjustable severity control. On mobile it opens directly as an AR experience using the rear camera, with no installation.
Design
Interaction centred on a small set of filters and a single severity control, so that comparison between conditions is immediate. The camera view is the primary surface, which means the interface had to stay out of the way while remaining operable on a phone.
Context
Built as part of my work as a Research Assistant in Creative Technologies and Design, and tested on mobile hardware.
Relevance
Accessibility treated as a design requirement rather than a checklist item, and an interface designed for two very different screen contexts.

VR Regalia: Museum Interaction Prototype

Meta Quest 3 · Unity · embodied interaction

Problem
A museum setting asks visitors to look closely at objects, but in VR there is no way to lean in or read a label. How should a virtual exhibit communicate detail without breaking the sense of standing in a room?
Work
A dark themed virtual gallery containing five mannequins displaying prototype Powwow inspired regalia. The visitor approaches a garment and points at interactable regions with the controllers. Haptic feedback confirms the target, and pressing the trigger reveals text describing that specific part of the regalia.
Design
Interaction built on proximity, ray targeting, and multimodal confirmation. The haptic pulse does the work that a cursor would do on a screen, signalling that a region is selectable before the visitor commits. Information is revealed on demand rather than displayed continuously, so the gallery stays visually quiet and the visitor controls the pace of reading.
Role
Lead of the Research Assistant position and designer of the VR prototype, built for Meta Quest 3 in Unity. The regalia are original prototypes rather than reproductions of specific historical garments.

Creative Coding Practice

Unity and Arduino · computational media as artistic material

Practice
Unity with C# for interactive scenes, physics, and real time behavior; Arduino with C++ for physical computing, sensors, and actuators. Physical and Embedded Interaction

Arduino based interfaces · CTCH 223 / CS 207

Problem
Physical interfaces expose interaction design because there is no screen to hide behind: a motor either responds predictably or it does not.
Work
Student projects in control of DC motors, an RGB LED strip, an interactive sensor system, and interactive sound design recreating classic game melodies, each requiring attention to feedback, affordance, timing, and tolerance for imprecise input.
Demonstration
The playlist records laboratory demonstrations, including a DC motor whose speed is controlled by a potentiometer, showing the working devices rather than describing them.

Student Game Design Projects

CTCH / DES 312 · Winter 2024 to Spring/Summer 2025

Context
I contributed to the course structure, assignments, evaluation criteria, and rubrics for Introduction to Computer Game and VR Design, and taught students through the full production pipeline from concept development to implementation.
Method
Structured project based learning with mentorship, integrating design, programming, storytelling, and user interaction. Students were asked to reason about the player's experience of what they built, not only about whether it ran.
Outcome
Student work across five terms, organized chronologically to show progressive skill development. In Winter 2025, recorded demonstrations improved in audio fidelity following the acquisition of a specialized microphone.
Projects
Student games including Mystic Realms, Kimetsu, Dawndale Grove, and Chicken Coop, spanning narrative design, level design, and interactive mechanics.

3D Production Pipeline

From reference to engine ready asset

Problem

Work
Character modeling from primitives, proportioned block-out checked in four views, joint hierarchy, smooth skin binding, and inverse kinematics animation in Maya, exported via FBX to Unity for real time preview and verified in Blender.
